**Interpretation limits:**

- A current below-normal observation, an ordinal record rank, and a long-term trend are different statements.
- Compare Antarctic recent conditions with its full-record fit and start-date sensitivity together; a single line can obscure persistent departures.
- Provisional days and any monthly means containing them can change after nominal replacement. A completed month is not necessarily final.
- Missing historical windows are excluded; provider-filled days remain flagged. Strict exclusions can change the distribution of sampled years.
- Pointwise regression intervals exclude the full measurement and calibration error budget. Short or structured residual records can weaken inference.
- The January 2025 nominal transition and September 2026 fast-track transition need continuing validation; an endpoint check cannot separate sensor effects from climate variability.
- Sea-ice extent alone cannot establish thickness/volume change, anthropogenic attribution, a regime shift, or a future ice-free date.

A defensible monitoring update reports the data vintage, current anomaly, provisional status, eligible comparison sample, and trend uncertainty together.
